Recovery

Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity

Recommended actions to help AFC respond to IT outages and restore critical services safely.

Safety first: In a fire, flood or other physical emergency, follow site emergency procedures and protect people before equipment. Notify management and emergency services where appropriate.

Recommended recovery targets

Planning estimates only. AFC management and service owners must validate these targets against business impact, cost, backup capacity and vendor capability.

ServiceProposed recovery time (RTO)Proposed recovery point (RPO)
ERP/database server8 hours4 hours
Core network, firewall and VPN4 hours for critical linksLatest saved configuration; target 24 hours maximum
DHCP/DNS network services4 hoursLatest configuration backup
Public website8 hours24 hours for site content
User PCs and printers1–2 business daysStore user data in approved backed-up services

RTO is the target time to restore a service. RPO is the maximum target data-loss window measured in time.

Scenario response guide

Internet or VPN outage

  • Log a high-priority ticket and identify affected sites.
  • Check whether the fault is local or provider-wide; contact the ISP.
  • Use an approved backup circuit if available and verify ERP access after restoration.

ERP/database failure

  • Notify the IT Manager and business leads; record errors.
  • Restore to approved replacement infrastructure or recover from backup.
  • Confirm database integrity and application access with the system owner before reopening service.

Ransomware or suspected malware

  • Isolate affected devices from the network where safe and possible.
  • Report immediately; do not wipe devices or reconnect them.
  • Preserve evidence, assess the scope, rebuild from trusted sources and restore clean backups only after security clearance.

Router, switch or firewall failure

  • Record the site and services affected.
  • Use compatible replacement equipment and the latest approved configuration backup.
  • Verify VLANs, VPN tunnels, DHCP relay, QoS and local internet breakout.

Power outage, fire or flood

  • Follow safety procedures and keep staff away from unsafe equipment.
  • Use UPS protection for short-term power events where installed and supported.
  • Use protected off-site backups and supplier support to restore services at a safe location.

Website outage or compromise

  • Notify IT and preserve logs.
  • Restore clean website files and content, patch the service and review DMZ/firewall rules.
  • Check that internal systems were not exposed.

Backup and testing checklist

  • Back up ERP/database data, website content, DHCP/network settings and firewall/router/VPN configurations.
  • Where feasible, keep multiple backup copies, use different storage systems and maintain at least one protected off-site or offline/immutable copy.
  • Encrypt sensitive backups and restrict access.
  • Monitor backup jobs and investigate failures.
  • Test file/database restoration at least quarterly and after major changes; record the results and corrective actions.
  • Maintain an approved emergency contact list for IT, management, ISP, hardware suppliers and ERP support.

Recommended recovery order

  1. Confirm staff safety and decide whether to activate disaster recovery.
  2. Identify affected sites, systems and security risks; appoint an incident lead.
  3. Restore power and core connectivity, then firewall/routing and VPNs.
  4. Restore required network services such as DHCP relay and name resolution.
  5. Restore ERP/database and verify data integrity.
  6. Restore the public website separately in the DMZ.
  7. Test access from each site, ERP reachability, internet, Wi-Fi and printing.
  8. Communicate service status and complete a post-incident review.

Owner: IT Manager · Status: Proposed — recovery targets and backup arrangements require management approval and testing.
